# Research report: the "small start, huge growth" incremental, idle and factory niche on Steam (2023–2026)

**How this was checked:** the network policy blocked every page fetch I tried: Steam store and API, SteamDB, SteamSpy, Gamalytic, Wikipedia, PC Gamer, Kotaku, Dexerto, GameDiscoverCo and AppMagic. I didn't try to get around the block. So every number below comes from search-result snippets that I checked against the URL listed, not from reading the full page. Where the summaries disagreed, I show both figures. Anything without a source is marked UNVERIFIED.

**Boxleiter method (used where marked "BX"):** copies ≈ reviews × 30–50. Gross ≈ copies × base price. Net to the developer ≈ about 50% of gross, after Steam's 30% cut, discounts, regional prices, VAT and refunds. That 50% is a rule of thumb, not a sourced figure. One check against real data: A Game About Digging A Hole sold 1M+ copies with about 14–20k reviews, a ratio of roughly 55–70×. So cheap viral games tend to sit at the high end of the range or above it.

## 1. The "needle in a haystack" game is Needle In A Haystack Simulator

- **Developer:** Studio Bitdot, the label of solo developer Nas Nakarus (@NasNakarus on X). Publisher is Polden Publishing. Steam app 5159870.
  - Sources: https://store.steampowered.com/app/5159870/Needle_In_A_Haystack_Simulator/ , https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/the-steam-ripoff-market-is-so-bad-that-viral-game-needle-in-a-haystack-simulator-is-facing-8-copycats-before-release-and-has-slapped-original-on-itself-to-clarify/
- **Status:** not released yet. Release window is Q4 2026. A playtest in August 2026 drew about 23,000 players (GamesRadar, above). Recommended GPU is an RTX 2060 (https://gaming-st.com/news/needle-in-a-haystack-simulator-steam/).
- **Not found:** price, wishlists, peak concurrent players and revenue are UNVERIFIED, and there are no reviews or sales yet.
- **Gameplay:** one needle hidden among 5,000,000 physically simulated pieces of hay, first person. You start by picking through the hay piece by piece, then sell the valuables you find. With the money you buy shovels, ladders and "increasingly powerful tools", and the search escalates into something absurd.
- **How it went viral:**
  - In August 2026 Nas posted a teaser clip on X. It passed 40M views in one weekend (GamesRadar, above), reached about 48–49M within days (https://ixbt.games/en/news/2026/08/24/indi-razrabotcik-pokazal-simuliator-poiska-igolki-v-stoge-sena-i-sobral-48-millionov-prosmotrov.html , https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/08/26/27790.html) and ended above 50M views with over 200k likes (Dexerto snippet).
  - What made people share it:
    - The title is a literal idiom, so it needs no explanation.
    - Showing 5M simulated pieces of hay is a technical spectacle.
    - The goal looks impossible, and the developer admitted he has never found the needle himself. That invites people to try.
    - It is "meditative, repetitive", in a satisfying, ASMR-like way.
- **The clone wave:**
  - Roblox creators made 14 copies within 48 hours (https://gizmodo.com/man-teases-most-infuriating-game-ever-roblox-vultures-immediately-steal-his-idea-2000802874).
  - On Steam, 8–10 lookalikes appeared by September 23. Nas posted a joking "formal apology" to the Steam storefront, and the Steam page now says "The viral one. The one and only." (https://www.gamesradar.com/games/simulation/the-robloxification-of-steam-creator-of-viral-game-apologizes-after-slop-rip-offs-flood-valves-store/).
  - Clones that released before the original:
    - *Needle In A Haystack* by NoGlyph (app 5085740). Released 2026-09-29, co-op for up to 6 players, sell hay, build machines. Snippet showed 57% positive of 69 reviews.
    - *Needle In A Haystack – A Time For Goats* by Red Key Games. Released 2026-09-18 or 09-19; the sources disagree.
    - *Find The Needle* (app 5160800), which has a "Hay Factory".
    - *A Needle In A Haystack: Sorting Game*.
    - A "20,000 demo players" figure turned up but is attributed unclearly: UNVERIFIED.
  - The upgrade path you remembered (needle → hay bales → factory) most closely matches the factory and machine progression in these clones.

## 5. Design patterns of short (2–6 h) incrementals that sell at $3–10

1. **The title is the pitch.** Literal and absurd: "A Game About X", "Dwarf Eats Mountain", Needle In A Haystack. Someone should understand it from 3 seconds of a clip.
2. **Start pathetic, end godlike, and make the growth visible on screen.** Bare hands, then a shovel, then machines. The hole gets deeper, the black hole bigger, Nodebuster ends by destroying reality. The finale has to be a screen-filling spectacle.
3. **A tactile core action with heavy feedback.** Digging voxels, scratching cards, flipping coins, hay physics. It should be satisfying, almost ASMR.
4. **Fast upgrade rhythm.** Every 30–90 seconds early on (this timing is my own UNVERIFIED rule of thumb), using one big visible upgrade tree, as in Nodebuster.
5. **Move from active play to automation midway.** Helpers, auto-scratch, machines, drones.
6. **Variance and luck without real money.** Scratch cards and coin flips; reviewers praise that Scritchy Scratchy has "no exploitative design".
7. **A finite ending with secrets.** 1–5 hours, sold at $3–7 so reviews say "great value". Add hidden endings or lore.
8. **Optional co-op** for streamers and friend groups (NoGlyph's 6 players, Needle Simulator, Meccha).
9. **Distribution:**
   - A viral clip on X, TikTok or Shorts before the Steam page exists.
   - An itch.io or web prototype to validate the idea (Gamblers Table, Dwarf Eats Mountain on galaxy.click).
   - A demo or playtest plus Next Fest.
   - A 10–30% launch discount.
   - Many languages (12–18) and Steam Deck support.
10. **Risks:**
    - Clones arrive within days (Needle Simulator). Ship fast and label yourself the original.
    - Free-to-play item-drop games (Banana, Bongo Cat) earn concurrent players, not money.
    - Repetition fatigue is the most common complaint in reviews.
